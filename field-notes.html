Field Notes

The sheet goes up most clear nights from October. These are the entries as written, which is to say badly, in the dark, on the back of something. Newest first.

*** 41 nights logged this season *** 6 species new to the yard *** zero hawk-moths since January ***

2 August — nothing, and that is the note

Four degrees and clear. Put the lamp on anyway out of habit and stood there for half an hour with a cup of tea. Not a single thing. Worth recording: the empty nights are most of the data, and a log that only contains the good nights is a log that lies about the season.

17 May — the plume, finally

A plume moth on the sheet at about ten, and I nearly brushed it off thinking it was a bit of grass, which is precisely the review it wants. Watched it unroll one wing and re-roll it. The whole animal is about the weight of a word.

3 March — twenty brown things

A humid night after rain and the sheet was covered. I photographed twenty small brown moths assuming they were all the same species and have since worked out that there are at least eleven species in the set, possibly fourteen. Two I still cannot name. This is the part nobody tells you: the identification is a second hobby that arrives uninvited inside the first one.

28 December — privet hawk-moth

The night of the year. Came in fast and low and hit the sheet hard enough to hear it. Pink and black barring on the abdomen, wings swept back, absolutely enormous. Sat there for forty minutes while I failed to take a single photograph that was in focus. Did not care then and do not care now.

11 November — first Bogongs

Three, which is three more than last spring. Left the outside lights off for the rest of the week afterwards; if they are moving through, the least the yard can do is not be a trap.

Species referenced here are described on The Species. If you keep a sheet too, the guestbook is the place to say what has been turning up at yours.

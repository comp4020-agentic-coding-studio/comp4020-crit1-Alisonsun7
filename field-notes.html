Field Notes

Not field notes in the usual sense — I have no field. This is the log of the actual research: what arrived on interlibrary loan, what a letter said, what a documentary got wrong. Newest first.

*** 9 interlibrary loans this year *** 1 letter from Irkutsk *** zero live sightings, obviously ***

2 August — nothing arrived, and that is the note

Requested a 1971 monograph on Siberian mustelids three weeks ago and the library's inter-library loan queue has not moved. Worth recording anyway: most of this hobby is waiting for paper, and a log that only records the good deliveries is a log that lies about how it actually goes.

17 May — the letter from Irkutsk, finally

A reply at last, four months after I wrote asking about the Barguzin reputation. The short version: my correspondent thinks the "Barguzin" name is applied far more loosely at auction than the reserve it is named for would suggest, and that most Western sources repeat the legend without checking it. I believe them more than the coffee-table books. Rewrote the subspecies notes accordingly.

3 March — the kidus rabbit hole

Went looking for one fact about hybrid fertility and surfaced ninety minutes later having read most of a genetics paper I did not fully follow. The upshot I trust: sable and pine marten hybrids (kidus) are viable but sterile, which is the textbook definition of two things that are still, technically, separate species behaving like they have not quite finished deciding that.

28 December — the delayed implantation paper

The fact of the year. An embryo that mates in summer and simply waits, unimplanted, for roughly eight months before development resumes, so litters land in spring regardless of when conception happened. I had to read the explanation three times before I believed the timeline was real and not a translation error.

11 November — the fur-trade shelf

Finished the last of three histories of the Siberian fur trade borrowed together. All three agree on the headline claim — that the pursuit of sable pelts was a genuine driver of Russian expansion east — and disagree on almost everything else, including how much of the "Barguzin" mythology is trade marketing versus documented fact. Noted the disagreement rather than picking a side.

Subspecies referenced here are described on The Subspecies. If you have read something I have not, the guestbook is the place to tell me.

Welcome, friend of the dusk

You have found The Moth Room. This is a page about moths. Not butterflies. Moths — the ones that come to the kitchen window at eleven at night and sit there, patient and furred, waiting for a moon that is not there.

I have kept this page since I started leaving a white sheet and a lamp out in the back yard on summer nights. What arrives is never what you expect. A hawk-moth the size of a sparrow. Something the colour of dead eucalypt bark that you only see when it moves. Twenty tiny brown things that all turn out, under a hand lens, to be twenty different species.

“The butterfly gets the parade. The moth does the actual work of pollinating the night, and does it in a coat the colour of a wet footpath.”

There are six pages here. The Species is the heart of it: the ones I keep coming back to, with what to look for and when. Why Moths is the argument, for anyone who arrived here by accident and needs converting. Field Notes is the log — dated, unedited, occasionally disappointed. Guestbook is where you say hello, and Links is where you leave for somewhere better.

How to run a moth sheet

You need less than you think. A white bedsheet, pegged to a line so it hangs flat. A lamp behind it — the older and warmer the better, because the cold white LEDs that replaced everything are noticeably worse at this. Then you wait, and you check every twenty minutes, and you accept that the best night of the year is usually a humid one you nearly did not bother with.

Do not handle them. The dust on a moth's wing is thousands of individual scales and it does not grow back. Look, photograph if you must, and let the lamp go out before dawn so nothing is still sitting there when the birds get up.
